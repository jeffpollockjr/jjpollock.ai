How it works

What working together actually looks like

Four phases, each with a clear deliverable and an exit ramp. You are never locked into the next step.

1

Week 0 · free

The intro call

Thirty minutes, on me. I ask about your business: where the hours go, which tools you already pay for, and what you've tried with AI so far. You ask me anything.

You leave with an honest answer: whether AI is worth your money right now, and if so, a rough scope for an audit. If it isn't, I'll tell you that too, and point you at whatever is.

2

Weeks 1–2

The audit

I interview you and the three to five people who do the actual work - not just the org chart. I map your workflows as they really run, inventory your tools and data, and find where hours leak.

Every opportunity gets scored two ways: return on investment and feasibility with what you already have. Then I build one small prototype of the top opportunity, because seeing it work beats reading about it.

Deliverables: a written roadmap, a working prototype, and a readout meeting where we decide together what to build - or whether to stop here. The audit fee credits fully toward a build signed within 90 days.

3

Weeks 3–8

Build & train

Before a line gets built, we fix the scope in writing: one workflow, one success metric we both sign off on. That metric is how you'll judge me.

During the build you get a 30-minute check-in each week - short, scheduled, and in plain English. Everything is built in your accounts with your logins, so the system is legally and practically yours from day one.

Launch isn't the end. I train your team hands-on, write a plain-language playbook for the workflow, and stay close for two weeks of stabilization while it beds in.

4

Ongoing

You run it

The system is yours, and your team knows how to run it. I check in 30 days after launch, included, to make sure the success metric held.

From there, two honest options. Take it from here - that's a win for both of us. Or keep me on a month-to-month advisory for maintenance and the next automation. Either way, the last thing I'll ask is who else you know who could use this.
